The Local Problem

Rendered walls, western sun, and what gives out first

Parramatta's building stock has changed faster than almost anywhere in Sydney. Duplexes and townhouses have replaced a lot of the old fibro and brick-veneer, and nearly all of it lands on the same palette: rendered walls in off-white or mid-grey, dark aluminium windows, and a timber-look element somewhere on the entry to stop the whole thing reading as a concrete box.

That timber-look element is where the trouble starts. Spotted gum and merbau battens look right for about two summers here. Parramatta regularly runs past 40°C, and a west-facing entry wall takes that heat every afternoon from December through February — the oil flashes off, the boards check and cup, and by year three the owner is either back on a ladder with a tin of decking oil or living with grey, splitting battens on the most visible wall of the house.

Co-extruded WPC does the same visual job without the maintenance cycle. The wood fibre sits inside a sealed polymer cap, so UV, heat and rain never reach it. No oiling, no re-coating, no cupping — a hose-down once a year is the entire routine.
