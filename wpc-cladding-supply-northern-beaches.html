The Coastal Problem

What salt air does to the alternatives

Anything within a couple of kilometres of the water on the Northern Beaches is in a corrosion zone, and cladding is usually where it shows first. Hardwood battens on a Freshwater or Collaroy elevation grey off within a season, then the salt works into the checking and the boards start cupping away from their fixings.

Steel and aluminium battens trade one problem for another. The extrusion itself may hold, but the fixings, cut ends and any coating damage become corrosion points, and rust streaks down a rendered wall are almost impossible to clean off properly.

Co-extruded WPC has no exposed core and nothing for salt to attack. The cap is wrapped 360° around the board during manufacture, so there's no cut-end weak spot and no coating to breach. It's the same reason the board holds up around pools — chlorine and constant splash are chemically no worse than what it takes on a headland.

Where It Goes

Pool walls, beach-house renos and everything facing the water

The single most common Northern Beaches order is a pool surround wall. Chlorine, splash and permanent damp are the exact conditions that finish off timber and attack steel fixings, and a capped board just shrugs them off — no swelling, no rot, no rust bleed onto the coping.

The second is renovation cladding on 1960s and 70s beach houses. There's a lot of tired brick and painted fibro from Dee Why up to Newport, and cladding the street-facing wall is a far cheaper way to modernise it than rendering and repainting on a cycle.

Third is screening: side passages, plant enclosures and outdoor showers, where the board takes constant damp and gets hosed down without any of it mattering.

Northern Beaches Cladding FAQ

Questions we get from Northern Beaches.

Will WPC cladding survive Northern Beaches salt air?

Yes — it's the main reason people specify it here. The wood-fibre core is sealed inside a co-extruded polymer cap wrapped 360° around the board, so there's no exposed core and no cut-end weak spot for salt to attack, and no coating to breach the way there is on steel or aluminium battens.

Is it safe to use around a chlorinated pool?

Yes. Chlorine, splash and constant damp don't reach the core through the cap. No swelling, no rot, and no rust bleeding down onto the coping from corroded fixings — which is the usual failure on steel-framed pool walls near the coast.
